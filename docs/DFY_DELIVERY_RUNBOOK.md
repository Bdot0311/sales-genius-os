# DFY delivery runbook

**Status: approved 2026-11-05.** Operating document for the Managed rung.
Strategy and pricing live in [DFY_OFFER_SCOPE.md](DFY_OFFER_SCOPE.md).

This exists because the offer is approved and the thing most likely to be lost
is not the sale, it is the proof. Two steps below are irreversible if skipped:
capturing the client's baseline on day zero, and getting permission to name
them at signature. Both are cheap on day zero and impossible to recover at day
ninety.

---

## Seat rule

Five concurrent Managed clients, hard. Client six is offered Launch or a
waitlist seat. A seat frees only when a client completes their 90 days and does
not renew, or churns.

Track the five by name somewhere you look weekly. The cap fails quietly, by
one seat at a time, with a reason each time.

## Before the first paying client

The reliability pass from the scope document, section 8. Once, not per client:

- [ ] Sign in, sign up and password reset on a fresh account
- [ ] Connect a Google mailbox end to end
- [ ] Build an ICP, run a search, reveal contacts
- [ ] Load a sequence and send a real first touch to a seat we control
- [ ] Reply lands in the unified inbox, agent drafts a response
- [ ] Deliverability dashboard scores the mailbox

## Day 0 — intake and baseline

**The step that cannot be redone.** A case study is a before and an after. The
after accumulates on its own. The before exists only if it is written down
before anything changes, and no client remembers it accurately ninety days
later.

Capture, in writing:

**Their current outbound**
- What are they doing now, with which tools, run by whom
- Last 90 days: emails sent, replies, meetings booked, pipeline created, closed
- Current spend: tools, people, agency retainers

If they have no numbers at all, **record that explicitly**. "No measurement in
place" is itself a baseline, and "they could not tell us their reply rate
because nobody was tracking it" is a strong opening line in a case study.

**Their business**
- ICP in their own words
- The offer, and their three best past wins — this feeds the Payoff Bank, which
  is the only source the generator may cite
- Average deal size and sales cycle length

Deal size and cycle decide how long until a provable win, which decides whether
this client can produce proof inside the term at all. A twelve-month enterprise
cycle cannot. Know that before signing, not at day 90.

**Permission, at signature**

Get the naming rights signed on day zero. Marketing and legal approval inside a
company takes weeks, and asking at day 90 invites a no from someone who was
never part of the engagement.

- [ ] Named case study agreed
- [ ] Testimonial agreed, video or written
- [ ] Logo rights agreed
- [ ] Three warm introductions, due day 60

---

## Days 1 to 7 — build

- Domains registered and authenticated: SPF, DKIM, DMARC, MTA-STS
- Client buys Google Workspace mailboxes, roughly $7 each, billed to them
- Mailboxes connected, warmup started
- ICP built in the product, fit and intent
- Campaign Brief written from the day 0 intake: offer, pain by persona,
  forbidden claims
- Three sequences written to house style, run through the Email Quality Checker
- **Client approves the messaging before anything sends**

---

## Days 7 to 14 — warmup, no campaign sends

Mailboxes warm before they send. Nothing goes out in this window.

**Say this at the sale, not at day 8.** A client expecting sends on day one and
hearing about warmup in week two reads it as a stall. A client told at signature
that the first campaign lands around day 14 reads the same fact as competence.

---

## Days 14 to 30 — ramp

- Start at 10 to 15 campaign emails per mailbox per day
- Watch bounce rate, complaint rate and placement before increasing anything
- Increase only on healthy numbers, never on a schedule
- First positive replies are expected in this window

---

## Day 30 — the first win, and the first ask

Ask for the testimonial **at the first booked meeting**, not at the end.

That is the moment enthusiasm peaks. The same request at day 90 gets a polite
sentence, because by then it feels like a chore rather than a reaction.

Ask for specifics, not praise:
- What was outbound like before this
- What changed
- Which number moved, and by how much

Never draft it for them. Never invent one.

---

## Day 60 — referrals come due

Three warm introductions, per the agreement.

Make it one click: send them a short intro email they can forward, naming the
kind of company that would benefit. An ask with homework attached gets
postponed indefinitely.

---

## Day 90 — case study, then renew or hand over

- Write the case study against the day 0 baseline, with real numbers only
- Record the video testimonial
- Renew at $2,500, or transition to Launch and a self-serve plan

The transition is not a loss. The Managed seat frees for the next proof, and
the client becomes the SaaS user the offer exists to produce.

---

## In scope, out of scope

Agree this in writing per engagement. Outbound scope creep arrives as list
building and reply handling.

**In:** domains and authentication, mailbox setup and warmup, ICP, list
building, sequence writing, sending, reply QA before the agent responds,
monthly reporting through the white-label portal.

**Out:** calls of any kind — Outreign is email outbound only and the offer must
never imply otherwise. Also out: LinkedIn outreach, content, website work, CRM
cleanup, paid ads.

---

## Weekly cadence

Budget one to two hours per client per week. Beyond that the cap is wrong, not
the week.

- **Monday** — review replies, QA the agent's drafts before they send
- **Thursday** — refresh lists, iterate messaging on what the replies show
- **Monthly** — client report through the white-label portal

## Agreement clauses

Plain language for a lawyer to tighten. **Not legal advice and not a contract.**

1. **Term** — 90 days, then monthly
2. **Price** — $1,500/month founding cohort, $2,500/month thereafter
3. **Client provides** — domains or the budget for them, Workspace mailboxes,
   offer and ICP detail, and approval on messaging within two business days
4. **We provide** — the in-scope list above
5. **Case study** — client agrees to a named written case study using real
   metrics. They approve the wording once; approval not unreasonably withheld
6. **Testimonial** — recorded video or written quote, at day 90
7. **Logo** — right to display on the site and in materials
8. **Referrals** — three warm introductions by day 60
9. **No guarantee of results** — no meetings, replies or revenue are
   guaranteed. There is no baseline to price such a promise against, and one
   cannot be offered until the cohort produces it
9a. **Refund** — full refund of fees paid if the client cancels within the
    first 30 days, no reason required. After 30 days, unused whole months are
    refunded on cancellation. Excluded in both cases: domains, mailbox
    subscriptions and other third-party costs, which the client has bought and
    keeps. The client also keeps the domains, mailboxes, sequences and lists
    built for them.

    **The window is deliberate.** A promise to refund when it "does not work
    out" has no definition behind it, because no baseline exists yet to define
    working. Left open, a client can decide at day 90 that no meetings means it
    failed, and the engagement is refunded after three months of delivery, a
    spent founding seat and non-recoverable data costs. Thirty days caps that
    exposure at one month while still reversing the client's risk at the point
    they actually feel it, which is before anything has been proven.

    It also matches the 30-day money-back guarantee already offered on the
    self-serve plans, so the two do not contradict each other in a sales
    conversation.
10. **Mailbox suspension** — no replacement guarantee. Providers suspend
    mailboxes for sending behaviour and our supplier offers us no guarantee, so
    we cannot pass one on
11. **Domains** — the client's, in the client's name, theirs on termination

---

## Kill criteria

- Delivery exceeds **10 hours in any week** → stop selling, work the backlog
  down before taking another client
- **Two consecutive cohort clients** produce no positive replies in 30 days →
  the offer or the targeting is wrong. Stop and fix it before selling more
- A client's deal cycle is longer than the term → do not sign them into a
  founding seat, because they cannot produce proof inside it
